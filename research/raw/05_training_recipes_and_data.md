# 05 — Training recipes and data sources for a Gemma 4 SWE agent (LoRA, Kaggle "Gemma 4 Developer Agent")

Compiled 2026-10-16 from ~65 web searches/fetches (arXiv, HF dataset cards, GitHub issues/PRs, Unsloth and vLLM docs) and cross-checked against the teammate captures in `00_kaggle_overview_verbatim.md`, `00b_kaggle_discussion_notes.md`, `00c_getting_started_notebook_facts.md`. Every number carries a URL. Items I could not confirm from a primary source are tagged **UNVERIFIED**. The arithmetic I did myself is tagged **(my calc)**.

## 0. Competition facts that constrain the whole plan (read first)

These override the framing in the task brief ("E4B / 12B / 26B-A4B / 31B"):

1. **Allowed bases are only the four QAT checkpoints** `gemma-4-31b-it-qat-w4a16-ct`, `gemma-4-12b-it-qat-w4a16-ct`, `gemma-4-e4b-it-qat-w4a16-ct`, `gemma-4-e2b-it-qat-w4a16-ct`. **26B-A4B MoE is not allowed** (overview page, captured in `00_kaggle_overview_verbatim.md`; host thread 748287 in `00b`). So MoE-LoRA questions (Section E) are moot for submission; I still note the vLLM status for completeness.
2. **Adapters are trained on the bf16 -it checkpoints and applied by the scorer on the QAT w4a16 model** (host answer in thread 748287, `00b`). Google's own card says the bf16 QAT export is `google/gemma-4-31B-it-qat-q4_0-unquantized` ("half-precision weights extracted from the QAT pipeline") and the `-w4a16-ct` file is "serialized in the compressed-tensors format for native, optimized inference with vLLM" — https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-31B-it-qat-w4a16-ct. One community adapter project explicitly trained on the `q4_0-unquantized` weights because the w4a16 file "has no quantized forward path" for training — https://huggingface.co/karthik-a/sparkflashxgemma4. **Recommendation: train on `-qat-q4_0-unquantized` (QAT-aware weights), not the plain `-it` bf16, to minimise the train/serve mismatch.**
3. **Scorer vLLM config**: vLLM 0.19.1, TP=4 on 4×L4 (96 GB total), `dtype=bfloat16`, `max_model_len=32768`, `tool_call_parser=reasoning_parser=gemma4`, `enable_lora=bool(adapters)`, `max_loras=len(adapters)`, **`max_lora_rank` = max detected adapter rank (default 128)** (`00c`). Submission zip ≤ **3 GiB** including adapters (`00c`). PEFT-format `adapter_config.json` + `adapter_model.safetensors` per adapter; different adapters per (sub)agent allowed, same base everywhere (`00_kaggle_overview_verbatim.md`).
4. **Runtime budget**: 12 h for ~120 hidden tasks inclusive of setup ⇒ ~5.5 min/task on 4×L4; 31B ≈ 35 tok/s, 12B ≈ 67 tok/s single-sequence; ~900 s per 1M tokens; successful 31B trajectories ≈ 40–50 LLM+tool calls and 300–350k tokens (`00b`, threads 746250/747857/748287). **ADK events compaction** kicks in at 14,336 tokens (keep last 5 events, compact every 15) (`00c`) — the model never sees a long raw history; it sees compacted summaries + last 5 events.
5. **Teachers**: Kaggle staff said distillation from external LLMs is allowed subject to the teacher's licence terms and the Apache-2.0 winner-release requirement (thread 742807, `00b`). Official rules quote: External Data must be "publicly available and equally accessible to use by all Participants … or meet[] the reasonableness criteria in Section 2.6.b" — https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/gemma-4-developer-agent/rules (as surfaced by search). Winning submission + source code must be Apache-2.0 (`00_kaggle_overview_verbatim.md`). ⇒ Prefer open-weight teachers (DeepSeek-V4, Qwen3.6/3.8, GLM, MiniMax) or Gemini (Google's own); Anthropic/OpenAI outputs carry ToS risk for an Apache-2.0 release (the thoughtworks dataset card itself flags this — https://huggingface.co/datasets/thoughtworks/agentic-coding-trajectories).
6. **Training data**: 129 public tasks (fastapi 67, rich 48, requests 13, httpx 1; 127 distinct base commits), fields include `problem_statement`, `patch`, `test_patch`, `base_commit`, `hints_text` (empty in all 129); 20.5 GiB of repo snapshots, 127 graphs, 127 embedding files — https://github.com/emiliodavola/kaggle-gemma-agent/pull/12 and Kaggle data page via search. Host: none of the training repos appear in the test set; test repos are private, mostly synchronous Python (`00b`, threads 747248/745998).

### A.2 Turning the 129 competition tasks into training signal

(a) **Teacher trajectory SFT.** Run an allowed teacher through the *official* `swegemma` harness (same 9 tools, same compaction, same 32k context) on the 129 tasks, verify with the repaired grader (thread 747248: only 43/129 pass gold in the stock public harness; community rebuilt grader gets 119/129 sound), keep resolved trajectories. In the harness, DeepSeek-V4-Pro resolved 58/124 and Qwen3.6-27B 59/123 vs Gemma 4 31B 54/124 (`00b`, thread 747168), so a single teacher pass yields only ~55–60 trajectories; with 4–8 samples per task at T≈0.7–1.0 and ≤3 kept per task (SWE-smith's cap) expect **~150–300 trajectories** from 129 tasks (my calc). Sample efficiency evidence: SWE-Gym got +13.6 points on SBV for 32B from **491** trajectories (https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21139v2); SWE-Dev reports 16.2% with 1,000 RFT trajectories (https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.193.pdf). Because teachers are *also* only ~45% in this harness, use them primarily to get *in-harness* format demonstrations and rely on external corpora (A.1) for volume.

(b) **Rejection-sampled self-trajectories (RFT / expert iteration).** Gemma 4 31B itself resolves 37–43% of the 129 under the harness (`00b`, threads 746250/747168) and 15% of tasks flip run-to-run, so 8 samples/task at T=1.0 should give resolved trajectories for ~55–60% of tasks (union of 4 runs was 73/123; `00b`). Precedent: SWE-Gym's MoatlessTools iterative RFT, 30 rollouts × 2 iterations, 7B 7.0→9.0→10.0% Lite; 32B 19.0→19.7→19.7%; cap 2 per instance — https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21139v2. Nebius RFT on Qwen2.5-72B: 10 rollouts, 6,548 passing trajectories, 1 epoch → 11.4%→20.5% SBV — https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03501. **Caveat**: SWE-Gym found mixing 868 on-policy 32B OpenHands trajectories with the 491 teacher ones *dropped* Lite from 15.3% to 8.7% ("self-improvement not yet working") — filter on-policy data hard (resolved, no test-file edits, no identical-call loops, short).

(c) **Localisation and edit supervision from gold patches.** Each task gives `patch` (gold files/hunks) and `test_patch` (FAIL_TO_PASS). Derive: file-level and symbol-level localisation targets (issue → files/symbols/line ranges), edit targets (issue + located file(s) → `edit_file(old_string,new_string)` calls reproducing the gold hunks), and a "patch hygiene" target (never touch test files; thread 747857 shows test-patch conflicts cost 2–8 tasks). This is exactly the SWE-Fixer retrieval/editing split (32.8% SBV with P2P filtering; https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.62/) and the SWE-Swiss multi-task SFT decomposition (Localization / Repair / Unit-test generation → 60.2% SBV at 32B; https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.790/). The karthik-a adapter did exactly this on the 129 tasks (104 train / 25 val): `main_lora` learns LOCATE/CAUSE/CHANGE + unified diff, `tool_lora` learns a localisation report derived from the diff — https://huggingface.co/karthik-a/sparkflashxgemma4 (no resolution-rate reported). Lexical baseline for localisation on these tasks: first grep hit correct 30%, grep+BM25 fusion top-5 61%, top-10 74%; 35/128 issues contain no code identifier (`00b`, thread 746250 comment) — so a trained localiser has real headroom.

## B. Converting SWE-agent / OpenHands / mini-swe-agent trajectories to the ADK + Gemma 4 format

**Evidence that scaffold mismatch costs points.** Open-SWE-Traces Table 5: training on OpenHands and evaluating under SWE-agent loses 2.1–5.6 SBV/SBM points and vice-versa 2.9–4.2; models trained on the SWE-agent-style harness transferred better — https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16038v1. Turn statistics differ a lot: OpenHands 94 assistant turns/trajectory (58 with thinking) vs SWE-agent 131 (75 with thinking), 234–313 tokens/turn (same source). The competition harness is **neither**: it has 9 ADK tools (`run_command`, `read_file(filepath,start_line,end_line)`, `edit_file(filepath,old_string,new_string,allow_multiple)`, `write_file`, `submit_patch`, `get_status`, `get_code_neighbors`, `search_similar_code`, `get_code_subgraph`), JSON tool results like `{'status':'ok','filepath','content'}`, and compaction (`00_kaggle_overview_verbatim.md`, `00c`). **Convert everything into the competition's own tool names and observation JSON**, then render with Gemma 4's chat template.

**Mapping rules (my synthesis from the tool definitions above):**
- OpenHands `execute_bash` / SWE-agent `bash` / mini-swe-agent single bash block → `run_command(command)`. Drop or rewrite commands that rely on scaffold-specific binaries (SWE-agent's `open`, `goto`, `scroll_down`, `edit N:M`; OpenHands' `execute_ipython_cell`).
- `str_replace_editor view path [start,end]` → `read_file(filepath, start_line, end_line)`; `str_replace_editor str_replace` → `edit_file(filepath, old_string, new_string)`; `create` → `write_file`; `insert` → `edit_file` with anchor context. SWE-Star's `think` tool has no equivalent → move its content into Gemma 4 thinking channel or drop.
- `submit` → `submit_patch()`. Drop turns that edit test files (the hidden `test_patch` will conflict).
- Observations: re-emit as the harness JSON shape; **truncate** long tool outputs the way the harness would. SWE-smith's student kept only the **5 most recent tool outputs** in context (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21798v2, App. F) and ADK compaction keeps the last 5 events and summarises the rest above 14,336 tokens (`00c`) — build training examples whose visible history matches that (summary block + last ≤5 events), otherwise you train on a context distribution the model will never see at test time.

**Gemma 4 template specifics.** Gemma 4 uses six special tokens for the tool-use lifecycle (tool-definition, tool-call, tool-response pairs) and wraps string literals in `<|"|>` — https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/prompt-formatting-gemma4 ; turn markers are `<|turn>user\n` / `<|turn>model\n` (Unsloth `train_on_responses_only` parts — https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train). Known template bugs to pin: transformers PR #45257 fixed `role: "tool"` handling and set EOS to the terminal `<tool_call|>` token — https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/pull/45257 ; the 26B template omitted the `<|turn>model` generation prompt when an assistant message has both content and tool_calls — https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it/discussions/20 . **Always render training text with the exact `apply_chat_template(tools=…)` from the tokenizer revision the scorer uses (Kaggle model "gemma-4 V2"), and diff a rendered sample against what vLLM 0.19.1's `gemma4` parser expects.** Thinking: when enabled, prior thoughts are dropped from history *except in tool-call turns, where they are kept* — https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it ; thinking ON was worth ~+20 points in the harness (23 vs 48–51/129; `00b`, thread 746250), so train **with** thinking content in tool-call turns (teacher thoughts or self-generated) rather than stripping it. Known parser hazard: vLLM's `gemma4` tool parser mangles `edit_file` calls whose `old_string` contains triple-quoted docstrings, after which the model retries up to 27× (`00b`, thread 747168) — add training examples that use `read_file` + smaller anchors instead of docstring-spanning `old_string`s.

**Loss masking.** Train only on assistant turns; exclude tool/environment output (SWE-Master: "excludes environment feedback obtained from Docker-based execution from the loss" — https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03411 ; HyperTool: loss on assistant turns incl. reasoning, observations excluded — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13663). Nebius additionally **masks assistant turns that triggered environment-formatting errors** so only valid actions shape the loss, and warns against dropping long trajectories wholesale because they carry negative examples of looping — https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03501 . In Unsloth: `train_on_responses_only(instruction_part="<|turn>user\n", response_part="<|turn>model\n")` — https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train ; verify that tool-response turns (role `tool`) also fall in the masked region after rendering (they render under a tool-response token pair, not `<|turn>model`, so check the mask on a sample).

## C. SFT recipe specifics (Gemma 4, LoRA/QLoRA, multi-turn agent data)

**Sequence length.** Trajectory token lengths from the literature: SWE-Gym resolved OpenHands trajectories mean **18,578 tokens (median 15,999, range 2,560–81,245)**, mean 39.9 messages — https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21139v2 Table 8; Nebius SWE-agent: avg 8,352 (resolved) / 15,241 (unresolved) — https://huggingface.co/datasets/nebius/SWE-agent-trajectories ; SWE-smith trained at 32,768 max (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21798v2), Skywork at 32,768 and notes >50-round trajectories overflow (https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19290v1), SWE-Master extended 32K→80K with YaRN (https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03411), Open-SWE-Traces trained at 131k (https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16038v1). **For this competition the harness context is 32k with compaction at 14k, so 16k–24k training windows cover the realistic visible-context distribution; train at 32k only for the uncompacted-prefix examples.** Packing: none of the SWE papers report packing; with per-example masking and very long samples, use no packing or Unsloth/TRL's padding-free (position-id-reset) packing only if you verify cross-example attention is blocked.

**Published hyperparameters (full FT unless noted):**
- SWE-Gym OpenHands 32B: torchtune full FT, LR 1e-4, ≤5 epochs, global batch 8, 32,768 ctx, 2–8×H100; MoatlessTools 32B: **LoRA rank 64 via Unsloth, LR 5e-4, batch 8, 5 epochs, single H100**; 7B full FT LR 2e-5 — https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21139v2.
- SWE-smith 32B: full FT, torchtune, LR 5e-5, 3 epochs (text renders "33", likely 3), 32,768 ctx, 2–8×H100 — https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21798v2.
- Skywork-SWE 32B: LR 5e-5, 3 epochs, AdamW wd 0.01, cosine, 32,768 ctx, **8×H800 for 12 h** on 8,209 trajectories — https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19290v1.
- Open-SWE-Traces (Qwen3-30B-A3B): LR 1e-5, batch 32, warmup 0.1, wd 0.01, cosine, 3 epochs, 131k ctx — https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16038v1.
- Unsloth Gemma 4 defaults: r=8/α=8 (text) or r=32/α=32 all-linear (vision), LR 2e-4 for short demos, "lower to 2e-5 for long runs", 1 epoch for a full run, adamw_8bit, `use_gradient_checkpointing="unsloth"` — https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train.
- karthik-a 31B QLoRA on the 129 tasks: r=16 (α=2r, rsLoRA), targets q/k/v/o/gate/up/down over 60 layers, vision excluded, LR 1e-4, 2 epochs, effective batch 8, NF4 + 8-bit optimizer, 2×T4, **120 s/step** — https://huggingface.co/karthik-a/sparkflashxgemma4.

**LoRA rank/targets.** Evidence for all-linear over attention-only in agent SFT is indirect (Unsloth benchmarks and SWE-Gym's Moatless LoRA both use all linear layers). Constraints here: scorer default `max_lora_rank=128` and the 3 GiB zip (a rank-128 all-linear adapter on 31B is ~1–2 GB bf16; `00c`). **Gemma 4 gotcha**: five global-attention layers have no `v_proj` (keys reused as values), so an explicit `target_modules` list containing `v_proj` attaches to 25 instead of 30 layers in 26B and analogous behaviour should be checked for 31B/12B; `all-linear` is unaffected — https://github.com/unslothai/unsloth/issues/4907 . Save adapters with **PEFT ≥ 0.19.1** (earlier exports had transposed tensors in a Gemma 4 case — https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/50252) and exclude the vision tower (language-only `target_modules`; PEFT rejects `Gemma4ClippableLinear` — https://huggingface.co/karthik-a/sparkflashxgemma4).

**Data-scale evidence (what N buys you).**
- 491 trajectories: 7B +8.8, 14B +12.4, 32B +13.6 SBV points (OpenHands) — https://arxiv.org/html/2412.21139v2.
- 1,000 RFT trajectories: 16.2% (SWE-Dev 7B) — https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.193.pdf.
- 5,016 (SWE-smith): 32B 40.2%, 7B 15.2%; 500-trajectory comparison setting 28.2% for 32B — https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21798v2.
- ~2k → beats 36.6%, 8.2k → 38.0% (Skywork, "clear log-linear trend") — https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19290v1.
- 1k→66k: log-linear, 8B reaches 39.0% (Kwai-Klear) — https://huggingface.co/datasets/Kwai-Klear/SWE-smith-mini_swe_agent_plus-trajectories-66k.
- 60k: 32B 57.8%, 4B 27.6% SFT, gains flatten beyond ~48k; difficulty filtering +3.6 — https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03411.
- SWE-smith ablations: repo diversity scales ~log(#repos); difficulty score has no strong correlation; single-repo specialisation 21.2% vs 13.6% on that repo — https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21798v2. (Relevant because the test repos are unknown: favour repo diversity over fastapi/rich depth.)

**Start from -it.** Every cited recipe starts from an instruct/coder-instruct checkpoint; here the base is fixed to `-it-qat`. Unsloth: if you want to preserve reasoning, keep ≥75% reasoning examples in the mix — https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train. **Forgetting the tool-call format**: no SWE-specific study found; general evidence says LoRA alone does not prevent forgetting and 5–20% replay of original-distribution data is the standard mitigation (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22337, https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/7343678.pdf). Practical mitigation here: (i) all training text uses the native Gemma 4 tool tokens via the official template; (ii) include 5–10% generic tool-calling turns (e.g. CodeAct/Apache-licensed function-calling data re-rendered to Gemma 4 format); (iii) gate every checkpoint on a 20–30 task harness smoke run that counts parser rejections ("mandatory input parameters are not present") before counting resolutions.

**Memory/throughput for Gemma 4 with Unsloth (training).**
- Official: E2B LoRA 8–10 GB; E4B LoRA 17 GB (another line says 10 GB); **31B QLoRA 22 GB**; 26B-A4B LoRA >40 GB (MoE QLoRA not recommended); Unsloth claims ~1.5× faster / ~60% less VRAM than FA2 — https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train.
- Unsloth context table (Llama-3.1-8B QLoRA r=32, bs1): 16 GB → 40,724 tokens; 24 GB → 78,475; 80 GB → 342,733; Llama-3.3-70B QLoRA on 80 GB → 89,389 tokens — https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/unsloth-benchmarks . **(my calc)** By interpolation a 31B dense QLoRA (~22 GB weights+overhead) on 80 GB should sustain ≥32k tokens at bs1 with Unsloth checkpointing; 12B QLoRA should sustain >64k. UNVERIFIED for Gemma 4 specifically — measure.
- Measured points: E4B QLoRA r=8, 2,048 ctx, 60 steps in 8m16s on A100-80GB, peak 10.12 GB (≈8 s/step, my calc) — https://vessl.ai/en/blog/fine-tune-gemma-4-on-cloud-gpu ; 31B NF4 QLoRA on 2×T4: 120 s/step at effective batch 8, short sequences — https://huggingface.co/karthik-a/sparkflashxgemma4 . Secondary (UNVERIFIED) H100 throughput: Llama-3.1-8B QLoRA ~6,500 tok/s at 4k ctx — https://yobitel.com/knowledge-base/unsloth ; one paper disputes Unsloth's highest claims and measured 11.7k tok/s on a 0.5B model — https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02609v1.
- **Estimate for 3k trajectories × 16k tokens = 48M tokens/epoch (my calc).** At an assumed 1,200–1,800 tok/s for 31B QLoRA on one H100 (8B ≈ 6.5k tok/s scaled by ~4× params; UNVERIFIED) → **7–11 h/epoch**; 12B at ~3–4k tok/s → **3.5–4.5 h/epoch**; E4B at ~8–10k tok/s → **~1.5 h/epoch**. Budget 2–3 epochs for 31B ⇒ ~1 GPU-day per run. T4 (fp16 only, 16 GB): E4B/E2B QLoRA at ≤4k context for smoke tests only; 31B does not fit at useful context on one T4.

## D. RL: what is feasible on one H100, and the cheaper alternatives

**Published RL costs (all far beyond one GPU):**
- DeepSWE (Qwen3-32B, rLLM, GRPO+, 4.5k R2E-Gym tasks): 64×H100 for 6 days ≈ **9,200 H100-h (my calc)**, 512 parallel Docker containers; 23%→42.2% SBV — https://www.together.ai/blog/deepswe , https://github.com/agentica-project/rllm/blob/main/examples/swe/README.md.
- SkyRL-Agent SA-SWE-32B: **4,601 H100-h** (vs 9,180 for DeepSWE), 39.4% SBV; 8B 3.6→9.4%, 14B 18.0→21.6% — https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16108 (cost figure via alphaxiv summary, UNVERIFIED against Table 2).
- Nebius (Qwen2.5-72B, DAPO, 65k→131k ctx): 128×H200; RFT 20.5% → RL 39.0% — https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03501.
- SWE-RL (Llama-3.3-70B, GRPO, 16k ctx, 1,600 steps × batch 512, UNVERIFIED aggregator numbers) → 41.0% — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18449.
- SWE-Master: 32 problems × 4 rollouts/batch, LR 1e-6, 150-turn cap; **4B: SFT 27.6% → RL 33.4%** — https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03411 (GPUs not stated).
- Agent Lightning v1.0 (Qwen3.5-9B, mini-SWE-agent, GRPO, 6k SWE-smith tasks): 41.8%→56.4% with "modest computing resources" (GPU count not stated; UNVERIFIED) — https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17528 . It wraps an existing harness through an LLM-endpoint proxy, which is the only framework in this list that could train through the ADK harness without re-implementing it.
- Frameworks: verl-agent (PPO/GRPO/DAPO/RLOO), ROLL and slime target multi-GPU clusters — https://www.turingpost.com/p/agent-rl-training-tools ; SkyRL works with SkyRL-train/VeRL/Tinker — https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16108 ; Unsloth supports GRPO/GSPO on Gemma 4 with `fast_inference=False` (no vLLM colocated; E2B Sudoku notebook) — https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train.

**Single-H100 verdict.** Online GRPO on 31B is infeasible (no room for policy + rollouts; the rollouts themselves need a vLLM instance and CPU-heavy Docker sandboxes). On E4B/12B with LoRA it is *technically* possible with Unsloth GRPO or Agent Lightning + vLLM (`VLLM_ALLOW_RUNTIME_LORA_UPDATING` with `load_inplace: true` is designed for exactly this async-RL loop — https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/lora.html), but each SWE rollout is ~5 min wall-clock in this harness (`00b`), so a batch of 32 problems × 4 rollouts ≈ 10 h on a single sequential sandbox unless you run ~16–32 sandboxes concurrently (CPU/RAM-bound, not GPU). Published small-model RL gains after a good SFT are +4–6 points (SWE-Master 4B) to +15 (Agent Lightning 9B) at hundreds of steps. **Within a 2-month, one-H100 budget, RL is a stretch goal after SFT+RFT; the expected-value play is offline.**

**Cheaper alternatives, in order of evidence:**
1. **Rejection-sampling fine-tuning / expert iteration** (SWE-Gym, Nebius RFT, SWE-smith all use it): +2–9 points per round at 7B–72B; one epoch over passing trajectories; cap 2–3 per instance.
2. **Outcome-filtered SFT with difficulty filtering** (keep tasks with mixed outcomes; SWE-Master +3.6).
3. **Soft-verified synthetic data** (SERA/Ai2: SFT-only, >200k soft-verified trajectories, "26× cheaper than RL", total ~$2k / 40 GPU-days at $2/H100-h) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20789 , cost figures via search summary UNVERIFIED.
4. **Preference optimisation on trajectory pairs**: SWE-Dev compared RFT vs KTO vs OREO (7B 23.4%) — https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.193.pdf ; ENTROPO (entropy-regularised multi-turn DPO/KTO for SWE agents, beats DPO/KTO with test-time compute) — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12434 ; Agentic-DPO builds state-level pairs from expert trajectories (tau-bench only) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10601 . Pairs are free once you have teacher-resolved vs self-failed trajectories on the same task.

**Reward design (for RL or for ranking RFT data):** binary test pass (everyone); SWE-RL's continuous **difflib.SequenceMatcher similarity to the gold patch, −1 for malformed output**, which beat a discrete exact-match reward (34.8% vs 29.0%, aggregator figures UNVERIFIED) — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18449 ; Nebius length penalty (0 below 10 steps, linear to the turn cap) — https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03501 ; SWE-Master: forced-submission on timeout gets 0.5× outcome, container failures masked — https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03411 ; add **format compliance** (parser-valid tool calls, no test-file edits, `submit_patch` called) and **localisation hit** (gold files opened/edited) as shaping terms — these map directly to the failure modes observed in the harness (`00b`).

## E. Multi-LoRA strategy and vLLM serving

**Specialists vs one generalist.** No paper directly ablates localiser/editor adapters against one generalist adapter on SWE-bench. Indirect evidence: multi-task SFT across Localization/Repair/Test-gen in one model works (SWE-Swiss 60.2% — https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.790/); a two-model retrieve-then-edit pipeline works (SWE-Fixer 32.8% — https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.62/); skill-specific adapters beat generic SWE adapters on SWE-Skills-Bench (Skill-to-LoRA — https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16769v1); the host explicitly suggested "more/larger specialist adapters" for small models (`00b`, thread 748287). Costs specific to this harness: every extra LLM call is expensive (a tool-free sub-agent took ~90 s for 3k tokens on 31B; `00b`), ParallelAgent with tool-using sub-agents is broken in ADK 1.36.1 (`00b`), and vLLM throughput degrades as the number of distinct adapters scheduled per batch grows (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00066 ; single-adapter overhead report https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/10062). With sequential single-task execution the adapter-switch cost is small; the real cost is extra calls. **Recommendation: one generalist "agent" adapter on the root LlmAgent, plus at most one small (r≤16) localiser adapter on a tool-free sub-agent that outputs candidate files/symbols from the issue text + grep/BM25 results, invoked once per task.**

**vLLM LoRA facts.** `--max-lora-rank` choices 1/8/16/32/64/128/256/320/512, default 16 — https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/configuration/engine_args/ ; set it to the max adapter rank, no higher (memory) — https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/lora.html ; the scorer sets it from detected adapter rank, default 128 (`00c`), so **r ≤ 128**. Multi-LoRA is per-request with "minimal overhead" per docs. **Gemma 4-specific bugs to test against vLLM 0.19.1 exactly:** dense Gemma 4 LoRA silently ignored (outputs byte-identical to base) reported on 0.19.2rc1 and 0.20.1, fix PR #39816 still open — https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/41754 (the scorer's 0.19.1 is *earlier* than the reported-broken builds; the sample submission with adapters scored 0.01 for one user (`00b`, thread 743140) — **verify adapter effect with a temperature-0 A/B on your local 0.19.1 before investing in training**); rank-32 all-layer LoRA on a compressed-tensors W4A16 base produced weak, non-reproducible outputs while rank-8 partial adapters were fine (OLMo-3.1-32B, vLLM 0.17.1/0.24.0, no maintainer response) — https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/50059 ; MoE one-shot kernel rejects rank >128 — https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/55158 . **MoE LoRA for Gemma-4-26B-A4B** (moot for submission): expert LoRA tensors were silently dropped, PR #50252 open with `needs-rebase`; serving requires `--enable-mixed-moe-lora-format` and `"is_3d_lora_weight": true`, PEFT ≥ 0.19.1 — https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/50252 ; Unsloth's expert targeting was fixed in PR #4913 (0.23% → 13.55% trainable at r=128) — https://github.com/unslothai/unsloth/issues/4907. **Merging**: not an option here (base must be the stock QAT checkpoint); PiSSA is incompatible (replaces base weights) and DoRA cannot be loaded by vLLM's LoRA path — https://huggingface.co/karthik-a/sparkflashxgemma4 . Stick to plain LoRA (rsLoRA scaling is fine, it is just α).

## F. Hardware plan (T4 16 GB local + one cloud H100 80 GB)

**On the T4 (fp16, no bf16; 16 GB):**
1. Data engineering: download/convert A.1 corpora to the competition tool schema (B), tokenise with the Gemma 4 V2 tokenizer, compute length histograms, dedupe by instance, filter test-file edits/git hacking/loops. CPU-bound.
2. Local harness + grader: run the repaired grader (`00b`, thread 747248), build the per-repo CV, run teacher/self rollouts against an *external* vLLM endpoint (teacher API or the H100 box) — the T4 only hosts the sandbox.
3. Template unit tests: render samples with `apply_chat_template(tools=…)`, round-trip through vLLM 0.19.1's `gemma4` parser (vLLM on T4 can serve E2B/E4B w4a16 for this), assert loss masks.
4. QLoRA smoke tests on E2B/E4B at ≤4k context (official: E2B LoRA 8–10 GB; E4B QLoRA ~10 GB at r=8–16, bs2 — https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train , https://www.gemma4.dev/guides/gemma-4-unsloth-qlora); note the T4 audio fp16 overflow issue is irrelevant for text. Do **not** attempt 12B/31B training on the T4 (karthik-a needed 2×T4 and 120 s/step at ~1k tokens).
5. Adapter A/B at temperature 0 on E4B w4a16 under vLLM 0.19.1 to prove adapters are actually applied (issue #41754 class of bugs).

**On the H100 (80 GB):**
6. **Which size**: 31B is the strongest base in-harness (43.5% vs 12B unknown; `00b`) but is slowest (35 tok/s; 5.5 min/task leaves ~40–50 calls). 12B runs ~1.9× faster, giving room for a localiser call and more retries. Train **both** a 12B and a 31B generalist adapter from the same data; pick by local CV *under the 5.5 min cap*. Given the 1-submission/day limit, decide by ~week 6.
7. **Method**: QLoRA (NF4, Unsloth) on `google/gemma-4-31B-it-qat-q4_0-unquantized` — 22 GB base footprint leaves ~55 GB for activations at 16–32k context with Unsloth checkpointing (https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train , https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/unsloth-benchmarks). bf16 LoRA on 31B (≈62 GB weights) leaves too little headroom for 16k+ sequences on 80 GB — use bf16 LoRA only for 12B/E4B. Train/serve mismatch (NF4 vs int4 g32) is small but real (https://huggingface.co/karthik-a/sparkflashxgemma4); the QAT-unquantized base minimises it.
8. **Settings**: r=32–64, α=r…2r, all language linear layers, vision off, LR 1e-4 (LoRA; 2e-5–5e-5 if you see format drift), 2 epochs, cosine, warmup 3%, bs1 × grad-accum 16, `max_seq_length` 24k (31B) / 32k (12B), adamw_8bit, response-only loss, checkpoint every 200 steps and smoke-eval 30 tasks per checkpoint.
9. **Expected hours** (from C, UNVERIFIED throughput): 3k × 16k tokens: 31B ≈ 7–11 h/epoch, 12B ≈ 3.5–4.5 h, E4B ≈ 1.5 h. Plan 3–4 full 31B runs (≈ 4 GPU-days) + 6–8 12B/E4B runs (≈ 2 GPU-days) + ablations.
10. **Generation/RFT on the same GPU**: alternate phases rather than colocate. Serve 31B w4a16 with vLLM (≈17–20 GB weights) alongside the sandbox farm for rollouts: single-H100 vLLM gives 31B bf16 **40 tok/s at concurrency 1, 375 tok/s at 16** (MTP drafter: 125/953) and 26B-A4B 177/975 — https://jarvislabs.ai/blog/gemma-4-mtp-vs-dflash-benchmark ; the w4a16 31B should sit between (bandwidth-bound; UNVERIFIED). At ~350k tokens per trajectory (`00b`) and ~400 tok/s aggregate, **8 rollouts × 129 tasks ≈ 360M tokens ≈ 250 h (my calc)** — too much; instead do 4 rollouts on the 129 tasks (~125 h) *or* restrict self-rollouts to 2k SWE-smith/SWE-rebench tasks with short caps and run teacher APIs for the rest. Use `--kv-cache-dtype fp8` and `--async-scheduling` (vLLM recipe — https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/recipes/en/stable/Google/Gemma4.html) and concurrency ≥16 sandboxes to reach the aggregate numbers.
11. **L4 reference points for the scorer**: 12B ≈ 67 tok/s, 31B ≈ 35 tok/s single-sequence on 4×L4 TP=4 (`00b`); no public L4 Gemma 4 benchmarks found; nearest 24 GB-card data: 12B AWQ-INT4 138.6 tok/s on RTX 4090 (https://markaicode.com/benchmarks/vllm-gemma-4-rtx-4090-latency-benchmark/), E4B QAT-int4 94 tok/s single / 662 at 8 concurrent on RTX 3090 Ti (https://github.com/VanLangSRI/vllm_gemma4_E4B). L4 is bandwidth-poorer than both, consistent with the host's numbers.
12. **Cost/time for 2 months (my calc)**: ~8 H100 GPU-days of training + ~6–8 GPU-days of generation ≈ 350–400 H100-hours; at $2–3/h ≈ **$700–1,200**. Kaggle free quota (~15 h/week L4×4) is reserved for end-to-end scorer-equivalent validation runs (one full 120-task run ≈ 11 h).

## G. Recommended default plan and top risks

**Data mix (target ≈ 4–6k training examples, ≈ 80–120M tokens after compaction-shaped rendering):**
- 40% external agent trajectories re-targeted to the ADK tool schema: SWE-smith-trajectories (MIT, 5k, Claude-generated — include but be ready to drop if the team decides Anthropic-ToS risk is unacceptable), Kwai-Klear 66k (MIT; subsample 1.5k short, resolved), Nebius OpenHands (CC BY; subsample 1.5k resolved with ≤40 turns), Open-SWE-Traces mini-swe-agent/Qwen3.8 subset (CC BY; 1k). Prefer trajectories ≤ 20k tokens and ≤ 40 turns (the harness budget), diverse repos (SWE-smith: diversity scales ~log #repos).
- 20% localisation + edit supervision: SWE-Fixer-Train-110K (MIT) subsample + gold-derived examples from the 129 tasks + from SWE-rebench/SWE-Gym gold patches (issue → files/symbols; file → `edit_file` call reproducing the hunk; "patch hygiene" negatives).
- 25% in-harness teacher trajectories (Gemini / DeepSeek-V4 / Qwen3.6 through `swegemma`, resolved, ≤3 per task) on the 129 tasks **plus ~500 SWE-rebench/SWE-Gym tasks run through the same harness** (so the model sees the real tools, JSON observations and compaction summaries).
- 10% self-RFT (31B/12B resolved rollouts, filtered as in A.2b), added in round 2.
- 5% replay: generic Gemma-4-formatted tool-calling turns + a few hundred plain instruction examples to protect the format.

**Training**: QLoRA r=32–64 all-linear language layers on `gemma-4-31B-it-qat-q4_0-unquantized` and bf16 LoRA on 12B, 2 epochs, LR 1e-4, 24k/32k context, response-only loss with error-turn masking; export PEFT ≥ 0.19.1 safetensors; validate under vLLM 0.19.1 TP=4 if possible (or TP=1 on the H100 with the same flags) with a temperature-0 adapter-vs-base A/B; CV on a repaired 129-task grader **and** a 100-task held-out set from other repos through the same harness (public-repo CV anti-correlates with LB; `00b`, thread 744319). Round 2: RFT on resolved self-rollouts; optional round 3: KTO/ENTROPO-style pairs from (teacher-resolved, self-failed) on the same tasks. Treat online GRPO as out of scope unless an Agent-Lightning-style proxy around `swegemma` is working by week 5.

**Top risks**
1. **Adapter not applied / mis-applied by the scorer's vLLM 0.19.1** (issue #41754 family; sample-submission-with-adapters failures in `00b`). Mitigation: temperature-0 A/B locally on the same version; keep rank ≤ 64; keep a no-adapter fallback submission.
2. **Train/serve mismatch** (NF4 training vs int4 g32 serving; rank-32 all-layer instability on compressed-tensors bases — issue #50059). Mitigation: train on the QAT-unquantized export, evaluate only on the w4a16 checkpoint, try r=16 partial-coverage adapters as a control.
3. **Context-distribution mismatch**: trajectories trained with full history vs ADK compaction at 14k/keep-5. Mitigation: render training samples compaction-shaped; keep trajectories short.
4. **Format regressions** (parser-rejected `edit_file`, missing `submit_patch`, test-file edits). Mitigation: masked error turns, explicit negatives, smoke-eval counting parser rejections per checkpoint.
5. **Generalisation to private repos**: fastapi-heavy dev set, CV/LB anti-correlation. Mitigation: repo-diverse external data; held-out other-repo CV through the harness.
6. **Licence/ToS of teacher-generated data** for the Apache-2.0 release. Mitigation: open-weight or Gemini teachers; keep a provenance manifest per training example.
7. **Time budget**: 12 h hard cap kills the whole submission; 31B leaves ~5.5 min/task. Mitigation: train for brevity (short trajectories), cap turns in eval_config, consider 12B if the adapter closes the gap.
8. **Noise**: ±5–8 tasks run-to-run at T=1.0 and ±0.04 on the LB (`00b`). Mitigation: multi-seed CV, McNemar tests before accepting a change.

**Unverified items worth 1–2 hours of direct checking**: exact `max_lora_rank`/`max_loras` behaviour of the scorer's `adk_submission.VllmConfig` with two adapters; whether vLLM 0.19.1 (not 0.19.2rc) applies dense Gemma 4 LoRA correctly; Unsloth QLoRA tokens/s for Gemma 4 31B/12B on H100 at 16k–24k context; licence fields of `SWE-Gym/OpenHands-Sampled-Trajectories`, `LogicStar/SWE-Star`, `R2E-Gym/*`; whether Skywork-SWE trajectories were ever released.
